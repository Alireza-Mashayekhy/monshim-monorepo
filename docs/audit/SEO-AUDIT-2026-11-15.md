## 1. Executive summary

### Verdict

Monshim's **on-page SEO craft is genuinely good** — better than most Iranian SaaS sites of this size. Metadata is centralised and consistent, canonicals are correct, slug handling is disciplined, barber profiles are SSR'd with real structured data, analytics are consent/interaction-gated thoughtfully, and the redirect table for the `barber → provider` rename was written by someone who understands link equity.

But the site is **not yet technically or structurally prepared to acquire organic traffic**, and the reason is not metadata — it is **architecture and market surface**:

1. **There is no public page a customer can land on to find a barber.** Every discovery URL (`/panel/explore`, `/cities/{slug}/barbers`) is behind a login wall *and* disallowed in robots.txt. The two marketing CTAs that say "پیدا کردن آرایشگاه" send an anonymous visitor straight to a login form. [V]
2. **Every unknown URL on the domain returns a redirect to `/login` instead of a 404.** This is a systemic, site-wide signal error affecting all typo'd, dead and previously-indexed URLs. [V — live]
3. **The entire indexable inventory is 13 URLs** (9 static + 2 barber profiles + 2 blog posts). [V — live sitemap] There is no service landing page, no city page, no neighbourhood page, no directory, no public search.
4. **Measurement is absent**: no Search Console verification artefact anywhere in the codebase, and GA4/Clarity fire only after first interaction, with **zero conversion events** defined. You currently cannot prove or disprove any of the above with data. [V]

In one sentence: **the plumbing is solid, but there is almost nothing to plumb, and the one door customers are told to walk through is locked.**

### A.5 The public-profile business model, as implemented

Eligibility is enforced in the backend, not the frontend — this is correct and important:

- **Activation** = one-time package purchase (`773,000 تومان`, `constants.ts`) **OR** ≥ 300 gateway-paid, non-refunded bookings. [V]
- **Slug is one-time locked** after first save (anti-churn, good for URL stability). [V]
- **Public visibility** additionally requires: `isApproved`, `isPubliclyVisible`, active user, active barber role, ≥ 1 active service. [V]
- Access without slug/activation → `notFound()`. [V]
- Route accepts numeric ID and `permanentRedirect`s (308) to the slug — a good URL-canonicalisation pattern that protects against duplicate numeric/slug indexing. [V]

**Consequence for SEO:** the number of indexable profile pages is capped by a *paid* gate. Organic supply therefore grows only when barbers buy (or reach 300 bookings). That is a legitimate business choice, but it means **profile-page SEO cannot be the primary growth engine in year one** and the strategy must lean on the demand-side landing pages instead (see §D).

### F-01 · Every unknown URL on the domain redirects to `/login` instead of returning 404

**Severity: Critical** · **Confidence: [V]** (code + live)

**Problem.** Any path that is (a) not in the public allowlist and (b) requested without auth cookies is 307-redirected to `/login?callbackUrl=…`. Because the gate runs in the proxy *before* routing, the framework's 404 handler is never reached for anonymous visitors. There is also **no `app/not-found.tsx`** at the root, so even authenticated users get Next's default (English, LTR) 404 shell.

**Root cause.** `isPublicAuthPath()` is a closed allowlist (`lib/auth.ts:84-108`) and the proxy's `else` branch is "redirect to login" rather than "let the request through and let the router decide" (`proxy.ts:58-88`). There is no distinction between *"this route exists but is private"* and *"this route does not exist"*. Upstream, the customer-explore pages were made private (comment at `lib/auth.ts:88-89`), which pushed `/explore` and `/cities/*` out of the allowlist too — so genuinely dead URLs and private URLs became indistinguishable.

**Affected routes.** Every 404 on the domain: typo'd URLs, deleted/unpublished barber slugs at the *brand* level, `/explore`, `/cities/*`, `/test`, retired campaign URLs, any URL ever shared before the rename.

**Evidence (live).**
- `GET /this-page-does-not-exist-abc` → lands on `https://monshiim.ir/login?callbackUrl=%2Fthis-page-does-not-exist-abc`, title `ورود با رمز عبور | منشیم`.
- `GET /explore` → `https://monshiim.ir/login?callbackUrl=%2Fexplore`.
- `GET /cities/tabriz/barbers` → `https://monshiim.ir/login?callbackUrl=%2Fcities%2Ftabriz%2Fbarbers`.
- Contrast: `/provider/<bogus-slug>` correctly renders the branded 404 (`(public-barber)/provider/[id]/not-found.tsx`) with `noIndex: true` — because `/provider` *is* in the allowlist (`auth.ts:95`). This proves the mechanism, and proves it is a **scope** problem, not a page problem.

**Business & SEO impact.**
- Dead URLs are reported as *"Page with redirect"*, never *"Not found (404)"* → they never expire normally from the index and equity is never cleanly consolidated.
- The redirect target `/login` is `Disallow`ed in robots.txt (`robots.ts:11`), so Google cannot even read the target's `noindex` → the classic *"Blocked by robots.txt"* loop: URL stays indexed, no signal can remove it.
- Users clicking a stale link hit a login wall with no explanation → measurable bounce/dilution of brand trust.
- 307 (temporary) tells Google the move is provisional, which is the opposite of intent for dead URLs.
- Crawl budget: every miss costs a redirect + a full login-page render.
- `callbackUrl` is attacker-controllable input rendered into login links (see §F security boundary) — worth reviewing independently.

**Recommended solution (P1).**
1. In the proxy, resolve *route existence* before gating: if there is no matching page, `NextResponse.next()` so the router emits a real 404.
2. Add `app/not-found.tsx` — branded, Persian, RTL, links to `/` and `/blog`, and (once it exists) to the public discovery page.
3. Keep the auth gate only for a **known private-route list** (or invert to "public allowlist + explicit private list"), not as a catch-all.
4. Explicitly 301 `/explore` → the future public discovery page (or 410 if it will never return).

**Does not require a business decision.**

### F-02 · No public, indexable discovery surface for customers — the entire demand side is login-walled

**Severity: Critical** · **Confidence: [V]**

**Problem.** A customer searching *"آرایشگاه مردانه در تهران"* has no Monshim URL to land on. `/panel/explore` (login-gated, `revalidate 300`) and `/cities/{slug}/barbers` (login-gated, robots-disallowed) are the only inventory views, and both are excluded from the sitemap by design (`sitemap.ts:21-23`).

**Root cause.** Two sequential product decisions, both defensible individually: (1) retire the public `/explore` page and move search inside the panel; (2) add `/cities` without ever making it public. The result is a *closed* marketplace: the supply side (profiles) is public only for paying barbers, and the aggregation layer (directory/city pages) was never built.

**Affected routes.** `/panel/explore`, `/cities/*/barbers`, and — critically — the conversion paths of `/` and `/online-provider-booking`.

**Evidence.**
- `lib/auth.ts:88-89` — explicit comment that `/explore` and `/cities/*` are now private.
- `robots.ts:10` — `/cities` disallowed; `sitemap.ts:21-23` — same, with justification.
- Live: all three URLs → login (`§F-01` evidence).
- Homepage/landing CTAs: grep of `components/marketing/home/*.tsx` yields exactly `href="/panel/explore"`, `/register`, `/provider-management`, plus route-config hrefs. Live `/online-provider-booking` renders *"پیدا کردن آرایشگاه"* → `https://monshiim.ir/panel/explore`. The homepage FAQ claims *"بدون نیاز به ثبت‌نام/ورود به حساب"* while the primary CTA requires exactly that — a UX contradiction that also means Google sees no crawl path into inventory.
- Backend capability already exists: `getPublicDirectory()` (approved+visible+active+≥1 service) and `findPublicCityBySlug()` (≤100 per city, 404 if empty) in `apps/api/src/barber/barber.service.ts`.

**Business & SEO impact.** This is the single largest commercial gap. All high-intent, high-volume Persian transactional queries (`رزرو نوبت آرایشگاه`, `آرایشگاه نزدیک من`, `آرایشگاه مردانه در {شهر}`) are unreachable. Monshim can only compete for *brand* and *salon-owner-software* queries today. It also caps the value of the 773,000 Toman profile package: a barber buys a page that nothing links to except the sitemap.

**Recommended solution (P2, needs business sign-off — see §F).** One public, SSR, ISR directory surface that reuses the existing endpoints, e.g. `/providers` or `/salons` with filters, plus `/cities/[slug]` only where `findPublicCityBySlug` returns ≥ N real barbers (see §D.2 for the data-gated rule). Remove `Disallow: /cities` only for the new public paths, keep it for the private ones.

### F-03 · Public profile URLs are not linked from any public page — profiles are sitemap-orphans

**Severity: Critical (for the paid product)** · **Confidence: [V]**

**Problem.** The only machine path to a profile is `sitemap.xml`. No marketing page, article, footer or header links to `/provider/*`. Inside the app, `barber-card.tsx` links to `/panel/provider/{id}` (private) — the public URL is never the in-app link target for customers.

**Root cause.** The customer journey was moved inside the authenticated panel, so every in-app barber link points at a panel route; the public route has no "home" page to be linked *from*.

**Evidence.** `grep href=` across `components/marketing/home/*.tsx` → 6 unique hrefs, none `/provider/`. Footer `SEO_LINKS` has 4 entries, all static (`marketing-links.tsx:53-74`). `blog/barber-slider.tsx:51-53` does link `/provider/{slug}` correctly — but only inside articles an editor creates; the 2 live articles contain none (verified by reading both live articles).

**Business & SEO impact.** PageRank-diffusion is nil; new profiles depend entirely on sitemap fetch + crawl. Sitemap-only discovery is slower (typically days–weeks) and gives profiles no contextual anchor text. For a **paid lifetime page**, "Google may find it eventually via the sitemap" is a weak sales promise.

**Recommended solution (P2).** Whatever F-02 produces, plus: a "تازه‌ترین آرایشگاه‌ها" module on `/` and on city pages, an embedded barber slider in relevant articles, and a public-facing link from each barber's own share widget to their profile (already exists — see F-04).

### F-04 · The public URL the product hands to barbers uses the retired `/barber/` path

**Severity: Critical (correctness) → High** · **Confidence: [C]**

**Problem.** `publicUrl` is generated as `{origin}/barber/{slug}`, but the real route and canonical are `/provider/{slug}`. `/barber/*` is a 301 in `proxy.ts:41`. So the URL displayed on the dashboard, copied by the barber, and pasted into an Instagram bio (explicitly taught in `/tutorials`: *"لینک رزرو سالن را در اینستاگرام و واتساپ بگذارید"*) is **not** the canonical URL — it is a redirect.

**Root cause.** The rename touched the routes and the proxy but not the API's URL builder; unit tests then encoded the old path, so the bug is now *protected by the test suite*.

**Evidence.**
- `apps/api/src/public-page/public-page.service.ts:142-153` → `` return `${frontUrl}/barber/${encodeURIComponent(slug)}` ``
- `apps/api/src/public-page/public-page.service.spec.ts:369, 487, 550` → assertions on `https://monshiim.ir/barber/aria-salon`
- `apps/web/proxy.ts:41` → `{ from: '/barber', to: '/provider' }`
- `apps/web/app/(dashboard)/dashboard/public-page/page.tsx:49` → `URL_HOST_PREFIX = …'/provider/'` **but** lines 85/114-116/216-232 display and copy `status.publicUrl` (the `/barber/` value) → the page validates one prefix and shows another.
- Live: `/barber/roya` serves the roya profile (post-redirect), i.e. it is not a 404 — consistent with the 301. Exact status not readable [C].

**Business & SEO impact.** Every shared brand link is a redirect hop: slower first paint for customers arriving from Instagram (the main acquisition channel), analytics/social-preview splits between two URLs, and a barber who edits the URL to `/provider/…` by hand gets a different string than the dashboard shows. Equity is preserved by the 301, so this is **correctness + conversion**, not a ranking catastrophe.

**Recommended solution (P1 — tiny, high-value).** Change the builder to `/provider/{slug}`, update the three spec expectations, and optionally keep a `canonicalUrl` field so the dashboard can display the canonical while the API can still emit legacy links if ever needed.

### F-05 · Zero `BlogPosting`/`Article` structured data, no breadcrumbs, no author, no `dateModified` on articles

**Severity: High** · **Confidence: [V]**

**Problem.** The only editorial channel has no article markup at all. Article pages render `<h1>`, an excerpt, a Jalali date, a cover, and the body — nothing else.

**Root cause.** The blog was built as a content *container* (`ArticleContent` handles `toc/faq/barberSlider/media/gallery` blocks) and the `FAQPage` injector was added inside it (`article-content.tsx:426`), but no page-level schema was ever attached.

**Evidence.** `grep -rn "BlogPosting\|'Article'" apps/web apps/api` → **0 matches**. `(marketing)/blog/[slug]/page.tsx:32-59` imports no `Breadcrumbs`, no `JsonLd`; the only navigational element is a plain `← بازگشت به بلاگ` link (line 33-35). `BlogArticle` entity has `publishedAt` but no `updatedAt` surfaced to the page.

**Business & SEO impact.** No article rich results, no author/entity attribution (weak for E-E-A-T and for AI answer engines choosing a citable source), no breadcrumb trail in SERPs, and no way for a crawler to distinguish "fresh" from "stale". For a strategy that must lean on content (because discovery is closed — F-02), this is the wrong thing to be missing.

**Recommended solution (P2).** Emit `BlogPosting` (`headline`, `image`, `datePublished`, `dateModified`, `author` → Person/Organization `@id`, `publisher` → `#organization`, `mainEntityOfPage`) + `BreadcrumbList` (خانه › بلاگ › مقاله), and render a visible breadcrumb + a real `modified` date.

### F-23 · Things that are in good shape (do not "fix" these)

1. **Canonical strategy** — one origin constant (`site-config.ts:3`), `metadataBase` set (`layout.tsx:40`), canonicals built from `path` (`seo.ts:31`), profiles self-canonicalise to the slug, numeric → slug via 308. No trailing-slash or query-param drift anywhere.
2. **robots.txt** — live output matches code byte-for-byte; private paths disallowed; explicit `OAI-SearchBot` allowance (a deliberate GEO decision, and a good one).
3. **Private-page indexing policy** — `noindex,follow:false` at the layout level for `(admin)`, `(auth)`, `(dashboard)`, `(panel)`, `(onboarding)`, plus per-page `noIndex` on explore/provider/deposit. Nothing private is indexable, and nothing public is accidentally noindexed. **I found no accidental noindex.**
4. **Auth/SEO boundary** — private routes are gated *and* noindexed; `X-User-Payload` is stripped from inbound requests (`proxy.ts:63, 69`); `/uploads/blog` gets `nosniff` + CSP sandbox (`main.ts`); no PII in public HTML (verified on both live profiles).
5. **Slug rules** — 3-60 chars, `[a-z0-9-]`, no edge/double hyphens, not all-numeric, reserved-word list, Persian-digit normalisation, one-time lock, identical validation on both sides of the wire. This is better than most production code I audit.
6. **On-demand revalidation** — secret + `timingSafeEqual`, path + tag invalidation, wired to profile approval/edit.
7. **Font loading** — single 111 KB variable woff2, `display: swap`, `preconnect` to the API origin when it differs.
8. **Accessibility-adjacent SEO** — semantic `<article>/<header>/<ol>/<details>`, `aria-current` breadcrumbs, reduced-motion-aware sliders, FAQ answers present in initial HTML.
9. **Anti-thin-page guard** — `findPublicCityBySlug` returns 404 at 0 barbers rather than an empty page. Whoever wrote that was thinking about indexing.

### D.1 The strategic picture

Monshim has two very different SEO problems wearing one hat:

### D.4 Internal linking: the cheapest available win

Current state: homepage → 3 destinations; footer → 4 static links; header → 6; blog → `/blog/{slug}`; profiles → `/` + `/pricing` + booking (private). **Nothing links to a profile except the sitemap and optional article sliders.**

A minimal, high-impact plan:
1. **Footer block: "آرایشگاه‌های منشیم"** — latest 5-10 profiles (real links, real anchor text = salon name + city). Instant fix for F-03.
2. **City/module cross-links** on `/online-provider-booking` ("آرایشگاه‌ها در تبریز") once city pages exist.
3. **Article → service page → barber list** chain, using the existing `barberSlider` block (already implemented, currently unused).
4. **Profile → sibling profiles** ("آرایشگاه‌های دیگر در تبریز") — high relevance, low effort, grows naturally with inventory.
5. **Breadcrumbs everywhere** (blog articles currently have none).

### D.5 GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) — realistic assessment

**Already strong:** a coherent `@id`-linked entity graph (`Organization` + `WebSite` in `layout.tsx:55-77`), one canonical brand spelling (`منشیم` / `Monshim`), consistent NAP data across title/H1/JSON-LD/visible text, a real eNAMAD trust seal (`marketing-footer.tsx:184-191`), social profiles in `sameAs`, explicit `OAI-SearchBot` allowance in robots.txt, FAQ answers present in the initial HTML, and full server-rendering (so non-JS crawlers see everything).

**What's missing for answer-engine citation:**
- **No citable demand-side pages.** An assistant asked *"چطور در تبریز آرایشگاه رزرو کنم؟"* has nothing on monshiim.ir to cite except a profile.
- **No author/entity attribution** on articles (see F-05) — the strongest predictor of *who* gets quoted.
- **No About page, no company facts page** (founding, location, team, coverage) — entities without a describable "about" are harder to summarise confidently.
- No `Organization.foundingDate` / `areaServed` / `contactPoint`.
- No `llms.txt` (optional, unproven — low priority, and proxy already excludes `/.well-known` for `ai-catalog.json`/`ard.json`/`security.txt` which **do not exist** — dead configuration).

**Do not promise** that any of this causes an AI system to cite Monshim. These improve machine-readability and verifiability; the outcome is not controllable.

## F. Questions requiring business decisions

These cannot be answered from code, and I have deliberately not decided them.

**F-1 · Is a public, indexable discovery surface allowed?**
The central question. Options: (a) full public directory + city pages (max SEO, changes the "login to browse" product stance); (b) public directory only, no city pages yet (safer); (c) keep everything private (then demand-side SEO is off the table by design, and F-03's value drops — the 773k package buys a sitemap-only page). **Every demand-side recommendation depends on this.**
*Related:* is there a competitive/abuse reason browsing requires login (scraping, competitor harvesting)? If so, a public read-only directory may still be acceptable — it is public information either way once profiles are indexed.

**F-2 · Which audience owns the homepage?**
Salon owners (revenue today) or customers (volume later)? The H1, title, and internal-link priorities follow from this.

**F-3 · Which services deserve their own page?**
Needs local market knowledge. My constraint: only build a page if it can carry real explanatory content, a real FAQ, and ≥ N barbers actually offering it. Suggested shortlist to validate: کوتاهی مو مردانه، اصلاح ریش، خدمات داماد، میکاپ عروس، ناخن کاری.

**F-4 · Which geographic markets, and what is the minimum inventory for a location page?**
Threshold is a judgement call (3? 5? 10? real barbers). Neighbourhood pages need a **structured field**, i.e. a schema change + data entry — approve or reject explicitly.

**F-5 · Which profiles may be indexed?**
Today: any active profile with a slug (`getPublicDirectory`). Options: (a) keep as-is; (b) quality floor (bio + portfolio + services); (c) index only paid/300-booking profiles (consistent with the business gate). Also: should below-floor profiles be `noindex,follow` (shareable, not indexed) or fully excluded?

**F-6 · Should barber-registration intent be indexable?**
`/provider-signup` is currently `Disallow`ed. Unblocking risks duplicate/thin form pages; the alternative is a dedicated indexable landing page for `ثبت نام آرایشگاه`. The signup funnel itself should stay `noindex`.

**F-7 · What does "300 paid bookings" mean for SEO supply?**
At current inventory (2 profiles), the paid gate is the only realistic path to new indexable pages. Is a lower SEO-oriented tier (e.g. indexable profile without the full management package) commercially acceptable? This directly caps the ceiling of profile-page SEO.

**F-8 · Is `/barber/{slug}` still used anywhere outside the codebase?**
QR codes, printed material, SMS templates, Instagram bios. Affects whether P1-3 needs a permanent legacy mapping in addition to fixing the builder.

**F-9 · Analytics trade-off.**
The interaction-gated loader is a legitimate privacy choice, but it structurally undercounts zero-interaction landings. Approve a relaxed rule for landing pages, or accept the blind spot (and say so in the SEO reporting).

## H. One-page summary for the owner

**Good news.** The SEO *engineering* is above average: correct canonicals, disciplined slugs, SSR everywhere that matters, thoughtful redirects, no accidental noindex, private data properly fenced, and profile pages that are genuinely well-built for a barber's "name + city" search.

**The three things holding organic growth back:**

1. **The site has no 404s.** Every wrong URL — for everyone without a login cookie — redirects to a login page that robots.txt forbids. Small fix, domain-wide consequence.
2. **Customers cannot browse.** Every "find a barber" button leads to a login wall, and `/cities` + `/panel` are blocked from crawlers. Monshim is a marketplace whose shelves are behind a door. Two barbers, two cities, 13 indexable URLs.
3. **You can't measure any of it.** No Search Console verification artefact in the codebase, GA4 loaded only after interaction, zero conversion events.

**What I would not change:** the metadata helper, the canonical strategy, the robots policy, the 301 table, the slug validation, the auth boundary. Those are done well; touching them risks regression for no gain.

**Decision needed before implementation:** F-1 (public discovery), F-2 (homepage audience), F-5 (profile indexing policy), F-7 (whether SEO supply stays behind the paywall).
